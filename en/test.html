Test, Calibration & Qualification: from Wafer to Zero Defects

Automotive silicon targets zero parts per million (ppm) defects. Following the production flow, this chapter covers wafer probe test and analog trimming, multi-temperature final test, AEC-Q100 reliability qualification, and the wettable-flank package that lets assembly lines inspect solder joints optically.

Device figures: PSoC™ 4 HVPA-144K datasheet 002-28660 Rev. *K (“DS”); reliability conditions: AEC-Q100 Rev. J.

1. Wafer Probe (CP) & Analog Trimming

Process variation makes resistor ratios, transistor matching and the bandgap voltage differ slightly on every die. Untrimmed, ADC offset and gain errors are far larger than battery sensing can tolerate. At CP the ATE measures each die, computes correction codes and stores them in non-volatile memory; at power-up hardware or boot code loads them into the analog registers.

A typical correction model (offset, gain, temperature coefficient): \[V_{\text{corr}} = G_{\text{trim}} \cdot (V_{\text{raw}} - V_{\text{off,trim}}) + k_T \cdot (T - T_0)\] With \(N\) bits over \(\pm R\), the trim step is \(\Delta = 2R/2^N\) and the quantisation residual is \(\sigma \approx \Delta/\sqrt{12}\); ATE measurement noise and out-of-range tail dies add to it.

2. Multi-Temperature Final Test (FT)

After packaging, final test repeats parametric measurements at several temperatures. Testing at room temperature alone misses defects that only appear at the extremes:

  • Cold (−40 °C): start-up timing, oscillator start, comparator and timing margins.
  • Room (+25 °C): baseline and full functional vectors.
  • Hot (+125 °C for Grade 1): leakage, data retention, analog drift.

Temperature coefficients of analog parameters are verified here, and compensation coefficients stored where needed.

4. Wettable-Flank QFN Package

The HVPA comes in a 32-QFN (6 × 6 mm) with wettable flanks (DS). On a standard QFN the sawn side faces are bare copper, so solder only wets the bottom pad and the joint is hidden under the package where automated optical inspection (AOI) cannot see it. A wettable flank adds a plated step on the side of each lead, so solder climbs it and forms a visible fillet that AOI can check from above.
