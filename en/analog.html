Precision Analog & High-Voltage Power: Circuit Schematics & Principles

In automotive battery management systems (BMS), the microcontroller analog front-end directly interfaces with noisy, high-voltage battery rails. Grounded on the Infineon PSoC™ 4 HVPA-144K architecture, this chapter explores transistor-level and op-amp level circuit schematics: 4-terminal Kelvin shunt sensing, chopper-stabilized PGAs, 2nd-order switched-capacitor ΔΣ modulators, and 42V-tolerant monolithic PMIC regulators.

1. 4-Terminal Kelvin Shunt Sensing & Anti-Aliasing RC Filter Circuit

Shunt resistors for automotive batteries have extremely low resistance (typically 100 µΩ). Standard 2-terminal resistors suffer from solder contact resistance (0.5 mΩ ~ 2 mΩ) and copper trace TCR (3900 ppm/°C) that destroy accuracy. Thus, a Kelvin 4-wire connection with symmetric differential and common-mode RC filtering is mandatory.

2. Chopper-Stabilized Instrumentation Amplifier (PGA) Circuit

When measuring vehicle sleep currents (e.g. 15 mA), the voltage across a 100 µΩ shunt is only 1.5 µV. Native CMOS input offset voltages (0.5 mV ~ 2 mV) and 1/f flicker noise would obliterate this signal. The PSoC™ 4 HVPA utilizes chopper stabilization to eliminate offset drift.
