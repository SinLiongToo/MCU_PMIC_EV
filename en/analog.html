Chapter 02 · Precision Analog & PMIC Subsystem

Precision Analog & High-Voltage Power: Microvolt Sensing & 42V Direct Feed

Automotive battery monitoring poses an extreme physical challenge: current dynamic range spans over 1,000,000:1 (from a few milliamps in vehicle parking sleep to 1500 Amps during engine cranking or high-power regenerative braking), all while enduring destructive 42V load dump spikes on the battery bus.

1. Precision Analog Subsystem (PACSS) Architecture

The PSoC™ 4 HVPA PACSS integrates two synchronized differential converter channels: the Current Channel (ISENSE) and the Voltage Channel (VSENSE).

2. Dual 16-bit Differential $\Delta\Sigma$ ADCs & Sinc³ Decimation

Unlike SAR ADCs that are prone to switching noise from inverters, Sigma-Delta converters oversample the input and shape quantization noise out of the band of interest.

Sinc³ Decimation Filter Transfer Function:
\[H(z) = \left( \frac{1 - z^{-D}}{1 - z^{-1}} \right)^3\] By setting the modulator clock to 1.536 MHz and decimation ratio \(D = 1536\), output data rate is 1 kHz. The periodic notches fall exactly at 50 Hz, 60 Hz, and their harmonics, providing over 80 dB of normal-mode power grid rejection.

3. Fast Hardware Automatic Gain Control (AGC)

Using a standard \(100\,\mu\Omega\) shunt, a 1500 A cranking surge yields 150 mV across the inputs, while a 15 mA standby current yields just 1.5 µV.

  • PGA Gain 1x (±150 mV FSR) covers heavy charging and engine start.
  • PGA Gain 64x (±2.3 mV FSR) magnifies microvolt standby currents.
  • Fast Look-Up Table AGC: Switches gain stages in < 5 µs without CPU overhead, ensuring zero clipping and complete Coulomb tracking.

4. Integrated 42V High-Voltage Regulator / PMIC

Eliminating external PMIC bucks and LDOs, the integrated high-voltage linear regulator accepts direct battery connections from 3.6V to 28V, and survives 42V ISO 7637-2 load dump transients with high PSRR.
