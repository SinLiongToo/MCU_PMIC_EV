Chapter 03 · Automotive BMS Applications

Automotive Battery Management: From Terminal Clamp to Vehicle Network

Whether in traditional 12V start-stop lead-acid batteries, 24V commercial haulers, 48V mild hybrids, or high-voltage electric vehicle Battery Disconnect Units (BDU), the microcontroller acts as the central hub of the Intelligent Battery Sensor (IBS).

1. Intelligent Battery Sensor (IBS) Hardware Architecture

Mounted directly onto the battery's negative pole clamp, the IBS integrates the MCU, shunt resistor, and protection circuitry on a single compact substrate.

2. State of Charge (SoC): Coulomb Counting & OCV Recalibration

\[\text{SoC}(t) = \text{SoC}(t_0) - \frac{1}{Q_n} \int_{t_0}^t \eta(I) \cdot I(\tau) \, d\tau\] A tiny uncalibrated offset of \(10\,\mu\text{V}\) across a \(100\,\mu\Omega\) shunt causes a 100 mA phantom current, yielding a 24 Ah drift after 10 days! PSoC 4 HVPA eliminates this drift via auto-zero chopping (< 0.5 µV offset) and Open-Circuit Voltage (OCV) table resets after prolonged vehicle parking.

3. State of Health (SoH) via Cold Crank Dynamic Impedance

During engine cranking, peak current spikes by hundreds of amperes, causing terminal voltage to sag. The dual ADCs capture \(\Delta V\) and \(\Delta I\) synchronously at 1 kHz: \[R_{\text{int}}(T) = \frac{V_{\text{pre-crank}} - V_{\text{min-crank}}}{I_{\text{peak-crank}} - I_{\text{idle}}}\] Tracking dynamic internal resistance over time reveals plate sulfation, dry-out, and impending battery death before stranded road calls happen.

4. In-Vehicle LIN 2.2A / SAE J2602 Physical Layer

The IBS communicates with the Body Control Module (BCM) or Engine Management Gateway via a robust single-wire LIN 2.2A bus with automatic baud-rate synchronization on byte 0x55 and enhanced checksum security.
