Functional Safety: ISO 26262, On-Chip Safety Mechanisms & FMEDA Metrics

Functional safety means that when a random hardware fault occurs, the system detects it and reaches a safe state. This chapter first lists the safety mechanisms and the formal safety claim in the HVPA datasheet, then explains shunt open-wire diagnosis and the challenge-response window watchdog, and finally the ISO 26262 SPFM, LFM and PMHF metrics.

Device figures: PSoC™ 4 HVPA-144K datasheet 002-28660 Rev. *K (“DS”); metric targets: ISO 26262-5:2018. Figures 6.1 and 6.2 illustrate principles; currents, thresholds and the polynomial are examples, not HVPA specifications.

1. HVPA Safety Claim & Safety Mechanisms

Datasheet wording: “The device is developed compliant to ISO 26262, meeting all process requirements for ASIL C and hardware architectural metrics for ASIL B.”
In other words, the development process meets ASIL C, and the chip’s own hardware architectural metrics reach ASIL B. Claiming ASIL C for a system needs additional system-level safety mechanisms and use of the chip according to its safety manual.

2. Shunt Open-Wire Diagnosis (Principle)

An IBS sees years of vibration and thermal cycling, so a shunt solder joint or sense wire can go open. An open input floats, and noise may land right in the “normal” reading range. A common detection method injects a tiny diagnostic current into the sense lines: with the shunt connected (tens of µΩ) the resulting drop is negligible; with an open circuit the inputs are pulled to the rails and a comparator flags the fault at once. The HVPA can also cross-check the measurement path with its two ADCs and the diagnostic channel (VDIAG).

  • Normal: e.g. \(I_{\text{diag}} = 50\,\text{nA}\) through \(100\,\mu\Omega\) gives \(5\,\text{pV}\), far below one LSB.
  • Open: the input becomes megaohm leakage, so \(I_{\text{diag}}\) pulls the positive input up and the negative input down, far beyond full scale.
  • Decision: a comparator or the ADC window compare detects the out-of-range value, raises a fault interrupt and reports to the BCM.

3. Challenge-Response Window Watchdog (CRWDT)

A basic watchdog only asks to be serviced in time, so code stuck in a small loop that contains the service instruction keeps succeeding. The HVPA has two watchdogs: one that runs in Deep Sleep, and a challenge-response watchdog (CRWDT). The CRWDT raises a timeout when serviced too soon, too late or with the wrong key, configurable as a reset or an interrupt, and the cause is recorded in the Reset Cause register (DS §1.2.4).

  • Lower window bound: catches a runaway loop servicing too often.
  • Upper bound: catches stuck code or overrunning tasks.
  • Keys: firmware must present the expected keys in order, often folding in self-test results to prove the tasks actually ran. The key format is in the TRM.

Try early, late and wrong-key service in the MCU Lab.

4. FMEDA & Hardware Architectural Metrics

ISO 26262-5 quantifies random hardware failures with an FMEDA (failure modes, effects and diagnostic analysis). Failure rates are expressed in FIT: one failure per \(10^9\) device-hours.

Single-point fault metric (SPFM): \[\text{SPFM} = 1 - \frac{\sum(\lambda_{\text{SPF}} + \lambda_{\text{RF}})}{\sum \lambda}\] Latent fault metric (LFM): \[\text{LFM} = 1 - \frac{\sum \lambda_{\text{MPF,latent}}}{\sum \lambda - \sum(\lambda_{\text{SPF}} + \lambda_{\text{RF}})}\] Here \(\lambda\) is the safety-related hardware failure rate; SPF are single-point faults, RF residual faults (the part not covered by a safety mechanism), and MPF,latent undetected latent multi-point faults.

Worked example: a current-measurement path totals 50 FIT. 40 FIT is covered 99% by ECC, redundant measurement and the watchdog; the other 10 FIT is covered 90%. Residual faults = 40×1% + 10×10% = 1.4 FIT, so SPFM = 1 − 1.4/50 = 97.2%, just meeting ASIL C. Raising the level depends mostly on better diagnostic coverage of the blocks with the largest failure rates.
