Chapter 06 · Functional Safety & Hardware Diagnostic Circuits

Functional Safety: ISO 26262 ASIL-C On-Chip Diagnostic Circuits

In automotive traction battery systems, if an open-circuit fracture develops in shunt solder joints due to chassis vibration, or if the CPU enters an infinite loop while falsely reporting safe currents, catastrophic thermal runaway or unexpected vehicle shutdown can ensue. ISO 26262 ASIL-C requires safety mechanisms to be implemented down to the silicon circuit level. This chapter examines shunt wire-break micro-current injection circuits, challenge-response LFSR watchdog matchers, and FMEDA random hardware failure metrics.

1. Shunt Resistor Solder Joint Wire-Break Diagnostic Circuit Principles

The Intelligent Battery Sensor (IBS) is exposed to harsh engine-bay vibrations and aggressive thermal cycling (-40°C ~ +125°C). Micro-cracks can develop between the low-ohmic shunt and PCB pads, eventually leading to an open circuit. Without load, floating ADC input pins pick up ambient EMI that may mimic valid discharge current, masking the fault. PSoC™ 4 HVPA PACSS integrates Active Micro-Current Injection Diagnostic Circuitry.

Operating Physics & Detection Mechanics

  • Normal Soldered State: Diagnostic switches Diag_EN close, injecting a minute \(50\,\text{nA}\) current into ISENSE_P while pulling \(50\,\text{nA}\) from ISENSE_N. With intact solder joints and a \(100\,\mu\Omega\) shunt, the induced offset voltage is negligible: \[\Delta V_{\text{diag}} = 50\,\text{nA} \times 100\,\mu\Omega = 5 \times 10^{-12}\text{ V} \quad (5\,\text{pV})\] This picovolt-level perturbation has zero measurable impact on microvolt shunt measurements.
  • Wire-Break Fault State: When a fatigue fracture opens the circuit, the shunt is disconnected, leaving input impedance determined solely by pin leakage (\(R_{\text{leak}} > 100\,\text{M}\Omega\)). The \(50\,\text{nA}\) current immediately charges ISENSE_P up to \(V_{\text{DDA}} = 3.3\text{V}\) and discharges ISENSE_N to \(V_{\text{SSA}} = 0\text{V}\).
  • Hardware Intercept: The differential window comparator detects rail saturation (\(|V_P - V_N| \approx 3.3\text{V}\)), firing an NMI interrupt in under 2 μs to alert the vehicle gateway.

2. Challenge-Response Window Watchdog Hardware LFSR Architecture

Standard watchdogs simply require periodic timer resets ("dog feeding"). If software locks into an erroneous loop that happens to contain the feed command, standard watchdogs fail to detect CPU malfunction. ISO 26262 ASIL-C requires a Challenge-Response Window Watchdog architecture.

3. FMEDA Quantitative Random Hardware Metrics (SPFM / LFM / FIT)

ISO 26262 Part 5 mandates quantitative verification of random hardware failures using Failure Modes, Effects, and Diagnostic Analysis (FMEDA), measured in FIT (Failures in Time, failures per \(10^9\) operating hours).

Single-Point Fault Metric (SPFM):
\[\text{SPFM} = \frac{\sum (\lambda_{\text{SPF}} + \lambda_{\text{RF}})_{\text{covered}}}{\sum \lambda_{\text{safety}}} \ge 97\% \quad (\text{ASIL-C Specification})\] Latent Fault Metric (LFM):
\[\text{LFM} = \frac{\sum \lambda_{\text{MPF, detected}}}{\sum \lambda_{\text{MPF}}} \ge 80\% \quad (\text{ASIL-C Specification})\]
